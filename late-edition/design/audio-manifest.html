01 WHAT THIS IS

Generation-ready prompts · phased to Tier 0 / 1 / 2 · pasteable into Venice studio

Every audio asset Late Edition needs, expressed as a Venice-ready prompt with length, loop behavior, and trigger. Generate as you reach the milestone that needs them; the prompts encode the noir audio direction from §14 of the main design doc so the city sounds like itself even across regen passes.

Phase pills mark when each asset is needed: T0 required for the vertical slice · T1 for the playable build · T2 shippable polish.

Global voice direction

The sound design is diegetic noir, not cinematic noir. Everything you hear should plausibly exist in the world: rain on awnings, a press in the basement, a sad bandstand two streets over. Avoid: orchestral swells, modern percussion (no kick drums, no electronic risers), digital plug-in reverb that doesn't exist in 1948, "epic" bass drops, anything that pushes the player at the screen instead of into the city.

"Mono in the streets, stereo in the bureau, silent at the deadline." — mix philosophy

Mix reference points

These are tonal references for prompt-writing, not stylistic copying — never name a specific artist or song in a generation prompt unless Venice allows it: prefer instrument + era + microphone + room descriptions.

  • Music feel: mid-century cool jazz quartet recorded in a small, treated room, ribbon microphones, light tape compression, no reverb beyond the room.
  • Ambient feel: field recordings on a Nagra reel-to-reel, slight tape hiss, mono street with stereo interior cuts.
  • VO feel: American mid-Atlantic radio voice, 1948, AM-band rolloff (200 Hz–6 kHz), light cabin/booth tone, no breath gating.

03 MUSIC STEMS

6 stems · adaptive mixing · brushes / bass / trumpet / piano · swells DOWN to deadline

The score is one quartet, mixed adaptively. Six stems at the same tempo (~76 bpm) and key (likely C minor) so they can be layered or stripped in real time. As deadline approaches, layers drop until only bass and rain remain at T-30s. After the press fires, the full quartet returns for the eight-bar tag.

05 RADIO ANNOUNCER VO

The only voice in the game · 60 lines for shipping · phased

The radio announcer is a character, not a system. His broadcasts comment on yesterday's edition; what you printed yesterday changes what he says tonight. He starts amused and ends afraid. Across a 30-edition run he goes from gentle ribbing to careful avoidance.

Character bible

  • Name (player-facing): "Eddie Marsh, your old friend at WLKE radio, 1290 on your dial."
  • Age: late 40s. Smokes. Drinks coffee with his bourbon.
  • Voice: American mid-Atlantic, lightly nasal, warm baritone, classic 1948 AM radio delivery — slightly compressed, faintly hissy.
  • Arc: Editions 1–10: amused, gossipy, slightly flirtatious with the news. Editions 11–20: notices the player is the same byline a lot. Editions 21–30: visibly nervous. Says less, leaves longer pauses.
  • Never: shouts, breaks character, addresses the player directly, says the player's paper's name aloud.

prompt
American male voice, mid-Atlantic accent, late 40s, warm baritone, lightly nasal, classic 1948 AM radio announcer delivery. Slightly slow pacing with intentional pauses. Recorded through a ribbon microphone with AM-band character: gentle high rolloff above 6kHz, gentle low rolloff below 200Hz, light room tone, faint tape hiss, slight broadcast compression. The voice is amused, world-weary, and slightly tired. Never shouts. Speaks as if alone in a booth at 2am on a Tuesday.
