01 THE PITCH

One-liner · Setup · What it isn't

You run the city's most dangerous newspaper. You don't print the truth — you print the version that makes you rich. Build a print empire on a real-time clock, dispatch reporters, photogs and tipsters across a fog-soaked city, defend the press from rival rackets, and ship a new edition every twelve in-game minutes. Miss your deadline and you eat the front page.

The setup. 1948. An unnamed harbor city. Four papers compete for the morning edition. Three are real. Yours is a front — a syndicate that uses the newsroom apparatus (sources, photographers, tip lines, the printing press) as the infrastructure of an information racket. Every story you print pulls strings on the streets: bumps a stock, runs a politician out of town, scares a rival into selling.

The fusion. Late Edition is a real-time strategy game where the resource you optimize is information and the production line is a literal printing press. It's a builder (your newsroom grows from one desk to a building, then to a syndicate), a base-defense game (the press is sacred and the goons know where it lives), and a real-time race against the print deadline that arrives whether you're ready or not.

What it isn't. It isn't a clicker, an idle game, or a 4X. There is no global map. There is one city, twenty-odd neighborhoods, and a clock that doesn't stop. Sessions are 30–90 minutes. The reward for playing well is not numbers going up but the slow, satisfying assembly of a print empire that, by edition №100, runs the town.

Why three.js. The city wants to be a small toy, not a map. You should be able to lean in, see lit windows, see the rain. We need cel-shaded geometry, hard ink outlines, volumetric fog, instanced rain — all the things WebGL is good at and a flat 2D engine isn't. Three.js gets us a navigable, expressive city block in days, not weeks.

03 VISUAL DIRECTION

Black ink, bone paper, one drop of bleed-red

Cel-shaded toon geometry with hard ink outlines, set inside a perpetual 4 a.m. fog. Everything in the world is rendered in newsprint values — paper, charcoal, lampblack — with a single red accent reserved for player concerns: hot leads, breaking deadlines, fresh blood. Color is not a vibe. Color is a verb.

Material language

Toon shader

3-band gradient map

Every world surface uses MeshToonMaterial with a 3-step gradient (shadow / mid / light) on a monochrome ramp. No PBR, no metalness, no glossy highlights. Light is information; reflectance is a distraction.

steps3

Ink outlines

Edges geometry · 25°

Hard 1px black outlines around every world object using EdgesGeometry with a 25° angle threshold. This is the single biggest visual signature; do not lose it under any conditions, even when zoomed out.

angle25°

Fog wall

Linear · 22 → 60u

Linear fog from 22 to 60 world units, matched to the sky color. This is also the fog of war: enemy units invisible past the fog plateau, so the screen always reads as a small lit stage in a much larger dark city.

cover~75%

Rain (instanced)

600 line segments

600 instanced line segments falling at slightly varied speeds. Cheap, always on, audibly suggested by the SFX layer. Intensity ramps with edition pressure — by deadline minute zero, it is sheeting.

count600

Halftone print

2D layer · 3px dot

UI documents, photos, and the loading screen get a 3px-radius halftone treatment to sell the broadsheet metaphor. The 3D viewport stays clean — halftone lives on the 2D corkboard layer only.

dot3px

Lit windows

Unlit emissive

Each building rolls a sparse grid of cream-yellow window quads, drawn unlit so they punch through the fog. About 40% are lit at any time. They flicker awake/asleep on a slow timer to suggest a living city.

lit ratio~40%

04 THE WORLD

The city · The year · The factions

The city. An unnamed harbor on the eastern seaboard, 1948. Twenty-three neighborhoods stitched along a river. Streetcars, drugstores, a fish market that runs the political machine, a port that doesn't sleep. Each neighborhood has a personality (the docks, the diamond row, the meatpacking strip, the radio district, the embassy quarter) and a dominant kind of story — labor strikes in the meatpacking strip, war-bond fraud at the docks, vice in the radio district.

The year. The war ended three years ago and nobody quite came home. Veterans are angry. The mayor is on his fourth ulcer. The waterfront is half organized, half on fire. Television is a rumor on Madison Avenue. Radio is still king. The city is the perfect crime: too small to hide in, too big to police.

The player. You inherited the paper from your father, who died under circumstances that the official paper of record (yours) reported as a heart attack. It wasn't.

The streetlight rule. The whole game is played in fog. The visible disk around the player's nearest building is small; everywhere else, the city is a rumor. You see what your sources let you see. Information is the only resource that matters; everything else is downstream of it.

Districts (illustrative)

The Docks

Labor · Vice · Port

Longshoremen, smuggling, the foreman who decides whose son works tomorrow. Stories: strike threats, missing cargo, union politics.

Diamond Row

Finance · Insurance

Jewelers, insurers, the back rooms where stocks are decided over scotch. Stories: fraud, jewel heists, insider whispers.

Hillside

Society · Politics

Old money. The mayor's set. Stories: scandals, divorces, donations that aren't really donations.

Radio Row

Vice · Entertainment

Clubs, broadcasters, the men who own the late-night dial. Stories: payola, drugs, vanished singers.

A shipping build would carry 18–24 of these. Each spawns leads of its own flavor at its own rate; covering a district well means stationing a bureau there.

05 THE CORE LOOP

The Print Cycle · 12 in-game minutes · everything is on it

The game runs on a print cycle. 12 in-game minutes between editions. When the deadline hits, whatever stories you've filed go to press; whatever leads are still in the field are lost. The cycle is the heartbeat. It does not pause. The clock on the HUD is the most important pixel on the screen.

Why a clock. Without the deadline this is a chore-list game. With it, every decision becomes a triage problem: do I send my last reporter for one more lead, or do I bring her home before she gets jumped? The clock is the antagonist. The rivals are accomplices.

Between editions. When the press fires, the world resets a little: stories print, money lands, ink depletes, heat from the day before settles, the city responds (some districts get angrier, some get safer). The first 30 seconds of every new edition are a debrief — a literal stack of newspaper pages laid out on the corkboard, telling you what happened.

06 RESOURCES

Five currencies · only one is money
LEADSRaw tip-offs. Spawn in districts at a rate determined by your coverage there. Decay after ~3 minutes if not picked up. A lead is a Vector3 with a flavor tag. Capacity is your reporters' carry-count.
STORIESVerified leads. A lead becomes a story once a photog and an editor have both touched it. Stories live in your archive until you commit them to the next edition. Some die if left too long (the news goes cold).
INKConsumable. Each story printed consumes ink based on its size (filler / column / front page). Ink is purchased from the docks district. Run out and you literally cannot print the edition.
CASHIncome. Stories generate cash on printing — modified by accuracy, placement, and the district's mood. Cash buys buildings, staff, ink, and bribes. The least interesting resource. Intentionally.
HEATReverse currency. Rises when you print stories that anger powerful people, when you run agents recklessly, when you publish a lie. High heat = rival raids, police investigations, source dry-up. Bleeds off slowly between editions. The thing you optimize against.

Resources are intentionally lopsided. Cash is plentiful; ink is constrained; heat is the real budget. A good edition isn't the one that makes the most money — it's the one that makes good money without crossing heat = 80.

08 STAFF

Six unit types · you hire, you assign, you bury them when it goes wrong

Reporter

Lead-gatherer

The unit you have most of. Walks to leads, picks them up, returns them home. Variable speed and carry-count. Three substats: nerve (resists intimidation), nose (lead quality), legs (move speed).

cost$usesscout · gather · interview

Photog

Verifier

Joins a reporter on a lead to photograph it. Verified leads print at 2x value. Photogs are slow and skittish; pair them with reporters with high nerve.

cost$$usesverify · evidence

Tipster

Passive intel

Stationed in a neighborhood. Surfaces hidden leads, names sources. Doesn't move. Cheap, fragile, indispensable. Lose three to the same rival in one edition and you've leaked your network.

cost$usesreveal · whisper

Editor

Throughput

Stationed at the Bureau. Increases the rate at which verified stories convert to print plates. One editor handles 4 stories per edition; queue more and they spoil.

cost$$usesprint plate

Fixer

Defense

The only combat-capable unit. Walks where you tell him. Stations on a building to defend it; intercepts goons. One fixer beats one goon; two goons beat one fixer. Not a hero.

cost$$$usesguard · escort

Stringer

Risk

A freelance reporter. Cheap, fast, expendable. Goes places staff reporters won't. Cannot verify, cannot be promoted, dies first. There when you need to chase a lead that's clearly a trap.

cost$usesscout · expendable

10 DEFENSE LAYER

Build → defend → upgrade · the press is sacred · the press is on fire

The press is a target. The Evening Star sends goon teams to your Bureau on a rising schedule (one team by edition №5, two by №10, three by №15). Heat increases the frequency. Goons don't kill staff; they smash plates, steal stories, and set fires. The press itself can burn for 90 seconds before being destroyed and the run ending.

Three answers. (1) Fixers stationed at the Bureau intercept goons before they breach. (2) Safehouses around the city soak up patrol pressure. (3) Bribing the cops — a discretionary cash sink — delays the next raid by an edition. None of these are permanent; defense is something you maintain.

It is not a tower defense. There are no lanes. Goons appear from district edges, walk to the bureau, and try to enter. You see them on the map only if you have a switchboard with line-of-sight, otherwise the first you know is a window breaking. Information about the threat is itself a defense resource.

The press as fail-state. The whole game has exactly one fail condition: the printing press is destroyed. Reporters die, branches burn, ink runs out — none of those end the run. They make the next edition harder. The press going down ends it. Make it the most expressive, most loved, most fragile object on screen.

12 CAMERA & CONTROLS

Orthographic isometric · WASD pan · keyboard-first like a proper RTS
CameraOrthographic, isometric (45° / 30°). Pan with WASD or edge-scroll. Zoom in two steps: City (default, see the whole bureau radius) and Block (close in on a single intersection, see lit windows and rain trails). Rotation in 90° steps with Q/E.
Touch (secondary)Designed to not lock out tablet play, not optimized for it. Pinch to zoom, two-finger drag to pan, tap to select, tap-and-hold to context order, two-finger tap for box-select toggle. The HUD corkboard is sized so every actionable element is ≥ 44px on a 1024-wide tablet. Hotkeys absent; commands surface as a radial menu on long-press.
SelectionClick to select; drag to box-select; double-click to select all of a type on screen. Right-click to issue a context-sensitive order (move, gather lead, escort, return to bureau).
Hotkeys1–9 control groups. Tab cycles staff. Space toggles slow-mo (50% speed for 5 real seconds, with a 30s cooldown — never pause; pause kills RTS texture). Enter opens the morning edition.
MinimapTop-right. Newspaper-folded layout — your bureau is the centerfold; districts radiate outward. Leads ping as red dots; rival units as black dots. Fog of war = literal newspaper smudge.
Deadline UIA typewriter strip across the top counts down to 00:00. At T-30s the strip turns bleed-red and the world's rain doubles.

14 AUDIO DIRECTION

Rain · typewriters · cool jazz · the muffled press underneath everything

Bed. Constant city ambience: rain on awnings, distant streetcars, a foghorn every 90 seconds. The Bureau adds the soft mechanical heartbeat of the press — present even when off. The first time the press goes silent is the first time the player notices it was there.

Music. Cool jazz quartet. Brushes on drums, upright bass, muted trumpet, the occasional piano. Music swells down as deadline approaches — the bed thins to just bass and rain at T-30s. After the print fires, the full quartet returns in a celebratory eight-bar tag.

Diegetic sounds. Typewriters when stories are being filed. A wet match for cigarettes. A phone in the bureau ringing means a hot lead. Goons coming sound like windshield wipers across linoleum. The press itself has a layered loop — three rates for three throughput tiers.

VO. No protagonist VO. The only voice in the game is a single radio announcer whose late-night broadcasts comment on yesterday's edition. He is funny, world-weary, and increasingly afraid of you.

15 THREE.JS TECH NOTES

For implementation · what we proved with the feel test · what's still TBD
RENDERERWebGLRenderer, pixelRatio capped at 2, antialias on. ClearColor matches fog color (0xc8c4bc) so the horizon is seamless. No post-processing in the MVP — keep the pipe simple.
CAMERAOrthographic. Frustum height ≈ 22 world units at City zoom, 10 at Block zoom. Lerp between zooms over 250ms with a custom ease-out cubic.
SHADERSMeshToonMaterial with a generated 3-step CanvasTexture as the gradient map. NearestFilter, no mipmaps. Re-use one gradient texture across the whole scene.
OUTLINESEdgesGeometry with a 25° angle threshold + LineSegments at linewidth: 1.2. Parent the outline to its mesh so it inherits transforms automatically. Inverted-hull is the fallback if we need thicker lines.
FOGTHREE.Fog (linear), 22–60 world units. Driven by a single uniform — by dialing the near plane we can ramp from damp to soup on deadline.
RAINOne LineSegments with a buffer of N pairs. Update positions on the CPU each frame; cheap at 600 lines, will likely raise to 1500 on capable hardware. Falls in screen-space coords adjusted for camera tilt.
CITYBlock grid (5×5 in the test → 20×20 at ship). Each tile holds 1–3 boxes + window quads. Procedural generation seeded per save. Total geometry under 80k triangles for a full ship-scale city.
PATHFINDINGA* over an explicit 2D grid (street vs block). Reporters never walk through buildings. Pre-bake the grid at city-gen time; recompute around buildings on placement. Re-use across all units.
SAVElocalStorage for the MVP. The save is a single JSON: bureau position, building array, staff array, archive, edition number, current cycle remaining ms.
PERF TARGET60fps on a 2020 MacBook Air at City zoom with 12 buildings, 24 units, 4 active leads, rain on. Profile early; the toon material isn't free.

17 DECISIONS LOCKED

Decided · do not relitigate without cause

Input — keyboard+mouse primary, touch not locked out

Locked

The control model is RTS-traditional: WASD pan, hotkeys, drag-select. Touch is a secondary, supported path, not the primary target — but the HUD and hit targets are sized so a tablet player can complete a run. Decisions that would break touch (hover-only states, tiny click targets) are out.

Multiplayer — out of MVP, designed-for later

Locked

MVP is browser-only single player. No backend. Saves go to localStorage. Architecture should not assume single-player but doesn't ship netcode. When backend lands (accounts + cloud saves), MP follows on the same wave. Treat the press, the city, and the edition cycle as authoritative-server-shaped from day one.

City — one canonical city for MVP, random later

Locked

Ship a single, hand-tuned canonical city: 18+ named districts, recognizable, evolving across runs. Procedural city generation is a future feature bundled with MP (where rival players need a fair, shared, randomized map). MVP city is built as data, not code — so the proc-gen layer slots in without touching gameplay systems.

Stories — hybrid (procedural background + authored arcs)

Locked · see §18

~95% of headlines are procedural — slot-grammar templates that draw flavor from district personality. ~5% are authored arcs: 4–6 episode story chains triggered by world state, with hand-written headlines and branching choices. Full spec in the next section.

18 STORY ENGINE

Hybrid: a river of procedural news with hand-authored eddies

A noir game lives or dies on whether the player remembers Edition №47. Pure procedural news is forgettable. Pure hand-authored content doesn't scale. The answer is a hybrid: procedural background news so the city always feels alive, plus a small library of hand-written arcs that surface when the world is ready for them.

Three tiers of story

WHISPERS — ~70%

Procedural · 1-column filler

Slot-grammar templates. One lead, one reporter, one verification, prints as a column-inch on page 6. Low cash, low heat, infinitely available. The white noise of a working newspaper. These keep the cash flowing.

cash$80–250heat0–4

INVESTIGATIONS — ~25%

Procedural · multi-step

Procedural but with depth: verifying one lead spawns a follow-up lead, which spawns a third. 3-step chains. Print as half-page features. Mid-game bread-and-butter. An investigation can be killed by the rival paper if they file it first.

cash$400–1200heat4–18

ARCS — ~5%

Authored · 4–6 episodes

Hand-written story chains. Triggered when world state crosses a threshold (e.g. Docks district heat > 60, no Observer coverage). Episodes unfold across editions, each with a 2–3-way branching choice. The stories you remember. Front-page only. The game ships with ~8.

cash$2000+heat20–50

Slot grammar (Whispers + Investigations)

HEADLINE := [DISTRICT_ADJ] [ACTOR] [VERB] [LOCATION] · [SUFFIX]?

DISTRICT_ADJ(Docks) := Longshoreman | Dockmaster | Foreman | Wharfside
DISTRICT_ADJ(Hillside) := Socialite | Heiress | Senator's son | Society matron
DISTRICT_ADJ(Diamond Row) := Jeweler | Broker | Insurer | Appraiser
DISTRICT_ADJ(Radio Row) := Bandleader | Crooner | Producer | Disc-jockey

VERB(vice) := caught at | seen with | photographed at | named in
VERB(violence) := beaten outside | shot at | threatened at | found at
VERB(money) := indicted over | linked to | bribing | embezzling from

SUFFIX := "; police silent" | "; mayor furious" | "; sources confirm" | ""

EXAMPLE: "Wharfside foreman caught at Riviera Club; mayor furious"

Each district carries its own actor pool and vice-flavor weights. Verbs are tagged by category (vice / violence / money); each category implies a base heat cost so headlines feel honest even when generated.
