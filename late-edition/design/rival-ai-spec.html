01 WHAT THIS DOC IS

Behavior · not animation · not pathfinding · not stats

Three rival papers share the city with the player. Each has its own newsroom, its own reporters, its own goons, its own appetite for blood. This document specifies how each one thinks: what it observes, what states it occupies, what triggers state transitions, and what actions it can take against the player and the world. The numbers in this doc are starting points; the structure is meant to survive playtesting.

The three rivals are intentionally asymmetric. The Herald barely thinks; the Evening Star thinks too much; the Observer thinks slowly. Together they form a triangle around the player: cold rival, hot rival, conscience rival. None of them is "the antagonist" — the antagonist is the deadline. The rivals are the texture of the world.

"Three rivals. Three pressures. One press." — design intent

02 SHARED SYSTEMS

What every rival has · what every rival sees · how every rival ticks

The Rival interface

Every rival is an instance of the same interface: a black-box object that exposes a single method, tick(world, dt), returning zero or more intents to be reconciled by the world simulation. Rivals never mutate the world directly; they emit intents (RAID_BUREAU, FILE_STORY, BRIBE_REPORTER, OFFER_DEAL) that the simulation applies in deterministic order at end-of-tick.

SensorsEach rival observes: their own state, their reporter positions, the public edition history (last 10), districts they have presence in, visible player leads (only ones in districts where the rival has a Bureau), and a noisy estimate of player heat (±20).
MemoryPer-rival ring buffer: last 20 intents emitted, last 10 editions' deltas (cash gained, raids attempted, raids succeeded). Drives "is this working?" self-evaluation.
Tick rateEach rival declares its own tick interval. Faster ticks = more reactive but more expensive. Herald 1/edition · Evening Star 1/20s · Observer 1/2 editions.
IntentsStrongly-typed records: {kind, target, edition_due, priority, source}. Reconciler resolves conflicts (e.g. two rivals raiding the same Bureau at the same time → priority wins, other rescheduled).
DeterminismAll randomness flows through a seeded RNG per rival. Given the same world state and seed, a rival makes the same decisions — critical for replay, telemetry, and bug repro.
DifficultyDifficulty is a multiplier on frequency, not on aggression-type. A higher difficulty means raids more often, not raids that ignore the rules.

The intent vocabulary

Every action a rival can take in the world is one of these:

FILE_STORY RAID_BUREAU RAID_BRANCH BRIBE_REPORTER POISON_LEAD SCOUT_DISTRICT OFFER_WIRE_DEAL OFFER_BUYOUT PARTNER_REQUEST PASS

PASS is always available and costs nothing. A rival that emits PASS for two ticks running is "quiet" — the world UI does not surface anything from them. This is intentional: dead air is a state.

03 THE HERALD

The paper of record · slow thinker · genteel knife

THE HERALD

ESTABLISHED · LOW AGGRESSION · HIGH REACH

The paper of record. Two-thirds of the city reads it at breakfast. Doesn't get its hands dirty — but is very good at letting you do its dirty work and then forgetting your name. The Herald is rarely an obstacle. The Herald is a long game.

State definitions

IDLEDefault. Files 2–3 procedural society/finance stories per edition matching Hillside/Diamond Row flavor. Does not pursue player. Generates ~$1k cash, ~5 heat ambient — invisible to the player except in the morning edition recap.
COURTTriggered when player heat > 60 for two consecutive editions. Sends a single in-game letter ("the editor would like to talk"). Offers WIRE_DEAL: player files for the Herald at 50% cash yield, in exchange for −20 heat immediate. Holds for 2 editions awaiting answer.
WIREDWire deal accepted. Player's story cash is halved; player's heat passively bleeds −4/edition extra. Persists until broken or BUYOUT. Stable, slow attrition.
PUNISHTriggered if player declines or breaks a deal, OR if heat > 80 with no Wire. Once per 3 editions, files a competing story that scoops one of the player's queued Investigation-tier stories. Never sends goons. Holds for 1 edition then cools to IDLE.
BUYOUTEndgame state. Only reachable from WIRED at edition ≥ 25 with player heat < 30. Offers $50k flat to absorb the paper. Acceptance = "Establishment ending." Rejection = Herald flips to PUNISH for the rest of the run.

Transition table

FROM IDLE → COURT: player_heat > 60 for 2 consec editions
IDLE → PUNISH: edition ≥ 10 AND player_heat > 80 AND not WIRED
COURT → WIRED: player accepts wire deal
COURT → PUNISH: player declines wire deal
COURT → IDLE: 2 editions pass with no response
PUNISH → IDLE: 1 edition cooldown after a scoop
WIRED → BUYOUT: edition ≥ 25 AND player_heat < 30
WIRED → PUNISH: player breaks deal (files a story the Herald flagged off-limits)

Authoring note: the Herald should feel like a person, not a system. When it courts you, the message is hand-written. When it scoops you, it does so with a specific story. The state machine is the spine; the dialogue is the flesh.

04 THE EVENING STAR

Yellow press · ticks fast · hits hard · doesn't read

THE EVENING STAR

YELLOW · HIGH AGGRESSION · MEDIUM REACH

Tabloid trash. Pulls leads out of the gutter and prints them as front pages. The Evening Star is the rival you'll fight — physically. They send goons. They bribe your staff. They scoop your investigations before you've left the bureau. They run hotter than they can sustain and burn out around edition 25 unless you've made them stronger by being weaker.

State definitions

SCAVENGEDefault. Sends own (invisible-to-player) reporters to grab leads in the same districts the player covers. Steals ~30% of leads spawning in shared districts. Files 4–6 Whisper-tier stories per edition.
RAIDDispatches a goon team toward the player's Bureau. Goon count scales with heat: 1 goon if heat 40–60, 2 if 60–80, 3 if > 80. Pathfinds from a district edge; visible only via switchboard line-of-sight. Resolves win/loss on contact with the press.
BRIBESends an envelope to a randomly-selected player reporter. Chance of turning them = (100 − reporter_nerve − reporter_morale) / 100. Turned reporters file their next lead for the Evening Star.
RUSHSaw a visible Investigation lead. Dispatches its own reporters to scoop it. If they file first, the player's lead becomes worthless ("scooped" badge in the HUD). Lasts until that specific lead resolves.
PANIC3 raids failed in a row (fixer killed them all). Triples raid frequency for 2 editions. After 2 editions, drops to BURNOUT. This is the player's punishment for being too strong.
BURNOUTForced quiet. Cannot RAID or BRIBE for 3 editions. Still files Whispers. Recovery state. Important: even the Evening Star has a cooldown — without one, late game is a Vietnam.

Transition table

FROM SCAVENGE → RAID: player_heat > 40 AND last_raid > 2 editions ago
SCAVENGE → BRIBE: player has > 3 reporters AND no_bribe_this_edition
SCAVENGE → RUSH: any visible Investigation lead in shared district
RAID → SCAVENGE: goon team resolved (succeeded or wiped)
RAID → PANIC: 3 consecutive raids wiped without damaging press
PANIC → BURNOUT: 2 editions in PANIC complete
BURNOUT → SCAVENGE: 3 editions in BURNOUT complete
BRIBE → SCAVENGE: bribe resolved (turned or refused)
RUSH → SCAVENGE: contested lead resolves

Design note: the Evening Star's job is to make the player defend. The RAID state is the only thing in the game that genuinely threatens the run, so it must be both legible (the player should see it coming, given the right buildings) and survivable (a well-managed defense should beat any single raid). PANIC is the relief valve — if the player is winning every fight, the world acknowledges and steps off.

05 THE DAILY OBSERVER

Worker-owned · broke · honest · slow · the thing you'll come to hate

THE DAILY OBSERVER

LABOR · NO AGGRESSION · LOW REACH

Worker-owned. Six reporters, one printing press, no money. They report the truth slowly and methodically and they are absolutely not afraid of you. The Observer never sends violence. The Observer files counter-narratives next to your lies and watches the city's heat against you climb. The Observer is the moral landscape of the game.

State definitions

INVESTIGATEDefault. Quietly works on a single multi-edition story matching the worst lie the player has printed recently. Takes 3 editions to complete. Reveals investigation progress only via a corkboard rumor (the player has to be listening).
PUBLISHFiles the counter-narrative on its own front page. Effects: +25 heat to player immediate · player's matching lie becomes "discredited" in the next edition (cash refund minus 50%) · player's source on that story is burned. One-edition state.
WARNSends a reporter to interview a player source mid-investigation. Result: one player lead in that district is "poisoned" — picking it up costs +5 heat for misinformation. Used when investigation is ongoing but player is about to commit.
DESPERATETriggered when Observer cash < $200. Sends the player a one-time PARTNER_REQUEST: $500 keeps the Observer alive for 5 editions in exchange for the player NOT printing in one district they nominate. Accepting prevents PUBLISH against the player for 5 editions.
SILENCEDTriggered when the player succeeds at framing the Observer (a Tier IV move). Observer cannot publish, warn, or investigate for 3 editions. Heat decays slower for the player while Observer is silenced (the city notices the silence).

Transition table

FROM INVESTIGATE → PUBLISH: 3 editions in INVESTIGATE complete on a player lie
INVESTIGATE → WARN: player picks up a lead the Observer is investigating
INVESTIGATE → DESPERATE: observer_cash < $200
PUBLISH → INVESTIGATE: publish resolved
WARN → INVESTIGATE: poison applied
DESPERATE → INVESTIGATE: partner accepted OR declined (does not loop)
* → SILENCED: player completes the "Frame the Observer" arc · cannot reach otherwise
SILENCED → INVESTIGATE: 3 editions pass

Tone note: the Observer should make the player feel watched, never threatened. Their stories appear on the morning corkboard as printed pages next to yours. If you publish a lie about the docks and the Observer publishes the truth next to it, the visual moment matters more than the heat penalty. Build the UI to make that beat land.

06 INTERACTION MATRIX

How rivals affect each other · because they're not in vacuums

Rivals share a city. When two rivals act on the same target in the same tick, the simulation resolves conflicts using this matrix. Empty cells mean no special interaction — both actions resolve independently. The intent reconciler runs after all rivals have ticked.

vs Herald vs Evening Star vs Observer
Herald acts — Files first if both file same story; Star is louder so Star wins on Whispers, Herald wins on Investigation+ Never directly. Herald may print a softer version of the Observer's story to absorb its impact (heat to player ×0.7)
Evening Star acts (see above) — Will RAID Observer if Observer is publishing against Star instead of player. Star burns down Observer = SILENCED state for Observer.
Observer acts (see above) (see above) —

The Star-vs-Observer raid is the only inter-rival violence. It's a beat that should happen ~once per long run and the player should hear about it on the radio announcer's broadcast.

07 DIFFICULTY CURVE

How pressure ramps · what each rival is doing across the 30-edition arc

Editions 1–5. Only the Evening Star is meaningfully present. Light scavenging, no raids yet. The player learns the loop without pressure. The Herald is invisible noise; the Observer hasn't been seen.

Editions 6–12. Evening Star starts raiding. Herald appears in COURT if player heat climbs. Observer makes first appearance around Ed. 10, quiet at first.

Editions 13–20. Full triangle pressure. Raids regular, Herald either WIRED (low pressure) or PUNISHing (steady scoops), Observer publishing counter-narratives. Player is forced into hard tradeoffs.

Editions 21–30. Endgame. Buyout window opens for Herald. Evening Star tips toward PANIC/BURNOUT cycles. Observer's investigations finally land. The "Your Father's Killer" arc fires somewhere in here, regardless of state, and closes the run.

08 PSEUDOCODE

Shape of the tick · not the implementation · for handoff
// Every rival implements this interface.
interface Rival {
  id: 'herald' | 'star' | 'observer';
  state: RivalState;
  memory: RingBuffer<Intent, 20>;
  rng: SeededRNG;
  tickInterval: number;            // ms between ticks

  observe(world: World): Observation;
  decide(obs: Observation, mem: Memory): Intent[];
  tick(world: World, dt: number): Intent[];
}

// Top-level loop, run by the world simulation each frame:
function stepRivals(world, dt) {
  const intents = [];
  for (const r of world.rivals) {
    r.accumulator += dt;
    if (r.accumulator >= r.tickInterval) {
      r.accumulator -= r.tickInterval;
      intents.push(...r.tick(world, r.tickInterval));
    }
  }
  reconcile(world, intents);       // apply in priority order
}

// Herald's tick is the simplest — one decision per edition.
function heraldTick(world) {
  const p = world.playerSnapshot();
  const me = world.heraldSnapshot();

  switch (me.state) {
    case 'IDLE':
      if (p.heatTrailing2 > 60) return [enter('COURT'), file('hillside', 2)];
      if (p.heat > 80 && world.edition >= 10) return [enter('PUNISH')];
      return [file('hillside', 2 + rng.int(0, 2))];

    case 'COURT':
      if (me.courtAgeEditions >= 2) return [enter('IDLE')];
      if (p.acceptedWire) return [enter('WIRED')];
      if (p.declinedWire) return [enter('PUNISH')];
      return [offerWireDeal(p)];

    case 'WIRED':
      if (world.edition >= 25 && p.heat < 30) return [enter('BUYOUT')];
      if (p.brokeDeal) return [enter('PUNISH')];
      return [collectWireDues()];

    case 'PUNISH':
      if (me.lastScoopEdition === world.edition) return [enter('IDLE')];
      return [scoopPlayerStory('investigation')];

    case 'BUYOUT':
      if (p.declinedBuyout) return [enter('PUNISH')];
      if (p.acceptedBuyout) return [triggerEnding('establishment')];
      return [offerBuyout(50_000)];
  }
}

// Evening Star ticks every 20s. Goon dispatch is its hot path.
function starTick(world) {
  const p = world.playerSnapshot();
  const me = world.starSnapshot();

  switch (me.state) {
    case 'SCAVENGE':
      if (p.heat > 40 && me.editionsSinceRaid > 1 && rng.chance(0.18))
        return [enter('RAID'), dispatchGoons(goonCount(p.heat))];
      if (p.reporterCount > 3 && !me.bribedThisEdition && rng.chance(0.08))
        return [enter('BRIBE'), bribeRandomReporter()];
      const inv = world.visibleInvestigationLead('star');
      if (inv) return [enter('RUSH'), rushLead(inv)];
      return [grabLead(districtSharedWithPlayer())];

    case 'RAID':
      if (me.consecutiveFailedRaids >= 3) return [enter('PANIC')];
      if (me.raidResolved) return [enter('SCAVENGE')];
      return [/* in-flight, nothing new */];

    case 'PANIC':
      if (me.editionsInPanic >= 2) return [enter('BURNOUT')];
      return [dispatchGoons(goonCount(p.heat) * 2)];

    case 'BURNOUT':
      if (me.editionsInBurnout >= 3) return [enter('SCAVENGE')];
      return [filewWhispers(4)];

    case 'BRIBE': case 'RUSH':
      if (me.subAgentResolved) return [enter('SCAVENGE')];
      return [];
  }
}

// Observer ticks every 2 editions. Investigations are slow on purpose.
function observerTick(world) {
  const p = world.playerSnapshot();
  const me = world.observerSnapshot();

  if (me.state === 'SILENCED') {
    if (me.editionsSilenced >= 3) return [enter('INVESTIGATE')];
    return [];
  }

  switch (me.state) {
    case 'INVESTIGATE':
      if (me.cash < 200) return [enter('DESPERATE'), sendPartnerRequest(p)];
      if (me.investigationAge >= 3 && p.recentLies.length) return [enter('PUBLISH')];
      if (p.touchedObserverLeadThisEdition) return [enter('WARN'), poisonLead()];
      return [advanceInvestigation(p.recentLies[0])];

    case 'PUBLISH':
      return [publishCounterNarrative(+25 /* heat */), enter('INVESTIGATE')];

    case 'WARN':
      return [enter('INVESTIGATE')];

    case 'DESPERATE':
      return [enter('INVESTIGATE')];
  }
}

Pseudocode is illustrative, not normative. The goal is the shape: every rival is a small function with a switch on state, a snapshot of the world, and an array of intents back. Stateless dispatch + immutable observations + deterministic RNG is the only contract that matters.

09 OPEN QUESTIONS

Knowable through playtest · cannot be answered on paper
How much does the player see of rival state?Today the doc says: invisible scavenging, visible goon raids (if switchboard), visible counter-narratives in morning edition, visible courtship via letter. Should the player see the Herald's state on a roster screen? Probably not — they should infer it. Confirm in playtest.
Should the Observer ever go violent?The Observer is a moral landscape, not a combat actor. But: if the Star raids the Observer and we never give the Observer a way to fight back, the bullying may feel gratuitous. Consider: a "fund the Observer" Tier IV upgrade that makes them harder to silence.
Multiple goon teams in flight?The doc allows up to 3 simultaneous goons but doesn't say if they can arrive from different directions. Recommend: yes, but only at heat > 80. Asymmetric raids are the late-game pressure release the player will remember.
Difficulty selector?Not in MVP. The current curve assumes a single default difficulty. Once the curve plays well, hang Easy/Hard off the same machine by scaling tick frequencies (Easy = tick rates ×1.5, Hard = ×0.7).
